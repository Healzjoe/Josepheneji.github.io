Data Analysis

Python projects in pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, and Plotly: cleaning messy data, finding patterns, and visualizing the results.

1. Movie Budgets and Box Office Revenue

pandas · data cleaning · describe() · datetime conversion

A dataset of 5,391 movies with production budget, worldwide gross, domestic gross, and release date. The currency columns were stored as text with $ signs and commas, so I removed those characters and converted them to numbers. I then converted Release_Date to a datetime type and checked the data for NaN values and duplicates.

Findings: The average production budget is about $31.1M, and the average worldwide gross is about $88.9M. Some movies grossed $0, and the top grosser reached about $2.78B. The spread is very wide, so the average hides a lot of variation.

2. Google Trends vs. Bitcoin, Tesla, and Unemployment

pandas · matplotlib · dual-axis charts · time series resampling

I compared Google search interest with real-world data: Bitcoin prices, Tesla's stock price, and unemployment rates. I built dual-axis charts so both series share one time axis, formatted the date ticks by year and month, and resampled daily Bitcoin prices to monthly values.

What it shows: Search interest often rises and falls alongside price and economic events, which makes search data useful as an early signal.
matplotlibTime seriesresample()
3. Google Play Store App Analytics

pandas · Plotly · data cleaning · groupby

I cleaned a Play Store dataset by removing duplicate rows, dropping rows with missing ratings, and converting Price and Installs from text to numbers. I then estimated revenue per app as price times installs and removed apps priced above $250.

Charts include a donut chart of content ratings, a bar chart of app counts by category, a horizontal bar chart of total downloads, a grouped bar chart of free vs. paid apps (log scale), and a box plot of paid-app revenue by category.

What it shows: Most categories are dominated by free apps, and paid apps have a few very high earners alongside many low earners.

5. Image Processing: RGB to Grayscale

NumPy · PIL · matplotlib · matrix multiplication

I loaded a color image as a NumPy array with shape (height, width, 3) and converted it to grayscale. Each pixel's RGB values are multiplied by the weights 0.2126, 0.7152, and 0.0722, the standard Rec. 601 luma weights, using a single matrix multiplication.

Why it matters: Converting an image is the same operation as a matrix product, so NumPy's linear algebra applies directly to image data.
